# TRF Compared to Other Funding Channels

STATE: **DRAFT**

People frequently ask how TRF compares to other funding mechanisms. There's
probably nothing quite like it in the world right now! (let us know if there
is)

Here are the most important differences:

- Run purely by volunteers. Not-for-profit structure. All costs are documented.
- TRF does not get a "cut" from any project.
- Funders cannot get any physical goods. Funded projects are required to make
  digital assets available under acceptable licenses. The community collectively
  helps identify digital public designs that are most relevant, and finances them.
- We may vote on different projects every season. So, TRF is not something that
  folks would use to encourage the same project every month, at-least initially.
  (see next point)
- TRF is a great fit for various cause directed funding. Everyone gets 1 vote,
  independent of contribution.  Hpwever, projects funded in the community have
  the option to transition from funded to long life.
- There is no notion of "investment". TRF and the community does not take any stake
  in any project, or venture.
- Helps builds, scale, maintain, document and study democratic structures. Our
  data will be the proof of effectiveness of our methods. e.g. voting database every
  season, and decision making data. As our community grows, this could be a valuable
  resource for many things.
- Our voting system has novelties - carrying over funds to future seasons is an
  interesting mechanism, so is our 2 phase voting. The split vote and first-past-the-post
  enable funding small, medium and large projects in one unified process.
- By Indians, For Indians and in India. No foreign contributions. No money crossing
  borders.
- Minimal overhead, enabled by UPI.

From the point of view of projects that have a hardware aspect, TRF is more
suitable to get to a prototype, or even manufacturing ready stage, improvements
to an existing design, etc. For any projects looking to go beyond this, with
actual production and delivery, other platforms will be more suitable.
Credibility built via TRF would come handy.

For majorly "data" based projects, TRF is useful to do a thorough job of
making an a good initial implementation at a small scale, and a community
around it.  Then go on to do great things by finding the right way to scale.

For purely software projects, TRF gives an initial push. Repeat funding after
a year (set number of seasons) remains a possibility. We also have the
concept of "Long life projects", refer to [NOMINATIONS.md](NOMINATIONS.md)
