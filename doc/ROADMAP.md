# Roadmap of The Rupee Fund

STATE: **DRAFT**

On the surface of it, as of October 2026, The Rupee Fund looks like a simple concept:
members come together, pool funds, nominate projects, vote on them and thus
fund projects.

The question that must be asked is - what do members gain out of this ? Our minimum
contribution is 15 INR, which is affordable to almost anyone who is likely to be
reading this.  In exchange for this contribution, members sign-up to find good projects,
and vote on them.  This "attention" is precious, and the most important currency of
The Rupee Fund, not the money.

Funded projects have clear publicly reusable deliverables, so that is obviously a
validation for individuals, and a potential compounding factor. Members also get clear
attribution of having backed a project in their profile. If a project delivers the goods,
its members _may_ get positive karma for something well done! However, if a project does
not deliver the promised public goods, then the members of the project risk getting
negative karma - from potentially a lot of voters!

Beyond that, we'd have reviewers also in our platform - who review projects prior to
listing. These folks would be "domain experts" who would be voluteering.  They also
get suitable karma for reviewing, flagging, and promoting good projects.

Initially the fund size may be useful, but not capable of funding more ambitious projects.
But we must ask - as the community scales up, what are the possibilities ? What should
we aspire for ? If we assume a median contribution of 100 rupees, here is what our
season pool looks like as a function of community size:

1. 10k members gets us to INR 30 lakh rupees per season
2. 30k members gets us to INR 1 core per season
3. 1 lakh members gets us to INR 3 core per season. 12 crores a year!

Actual numbers might be higher or lower, depending on the mix of contributions, but
almost everyone will agree that a few crores is a serious amount of money - without
even taking into account the sheer heft of the demographic powering such decisions.

But before we go that far, let's see what how people have been raising larger chunks
of funds publicly for serious projects, in India. Startups have been the rage for
a while, so we'll just look at them.

[Shark Tank India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_Tank_India)
is a popular TV series that has captured popular imagination. Here, popular "Sharks"
take potential stakes in ventures in exchange for funding. How much money ? According
to the typical Google "AI summary", sourced from multiple news and other sites:

- In Season 5, investors committed ~ 95 crore across 73 deals out of 152 pitches
- The last two seasons (4,5) of Shark Tank India ran for 2.5 months, each with
  52 episodes
- The Contestants do not get the money immediately after shaking hands on the show.
- Sharks and founders must sign formal sheets and share subscription/shareholder
  agreements.
- Timeline to Disbursement: The actual transfer of funds takes anywhere from 1
  month to over a year
- Not All Deals Close: A significant portion of handshake deals made on set may
  not end up getting funded
- Shark Commitments vs. Realized Funds: "For example, out of ₹40 crore committed
  by sharks in Season 1, roughly ₹17 crore was actually finalized and invested
  within the first year."
- Shark Tank India has funded hundreds of startups across five seasons, spanning
  sectors like food and beverage, consumer tech, health, and sustainable fashion

Compared to that, with The Rupee Fund, projects are whetted before they are
listed. Funding could be almost instant! So if we have crores to give, and no
stake to take, then who is the winner ? Answer : Free and Open Designs for _everything_.
Let the implications of that sink in for a minute.

PS: look at what Shark Tank India did _not_ fund, at
[their website](https://sharktankseason.com/shark-tank-india-season)!

And after that, imagine for a few minutes what we can achieve over a reasonable
amount of time, say 2 years.

What do you see happening in Season 20 though ? 5 years is long, but here are some
tantalizing prospective projects that might be fair game:

1. Reusable designs of home appliances that are free from any "corporate" control
   by virtue of initial design.  But that does not make manufacturing everyone's cup
   of tea. Thus, the project which gets funded can then go on and run funding campaigns
   on other platforms for devices that are end user repairable, serviceable, long
   lasting, free of control, etc. How would you like a water filter ?
2. Homegrown digital software solutions that don't track you, while remaining
   ethical businesses built on sustainable subscription models !
3. Hmm - maybe an operating system, funded over multiple seasons spread over years!
4. How about a truly open mobile phone, that does not track you, but is yet
   sustainable for everyone - vendors included ?
5. A freely reusable rocket design, or a vehicle etc that is truly "open" in a few
   crore rupees...
6. Silicon fabrication machinery that has a free process design kit and free
   of any control in says tens of crores - possibly funded over multiple seasons.
   Collaborators across universities and startups. OK ok - to be fair, this might
   take longer, but dream we can... can't we?

All said and done, TRF aims to be a true leveller for projects. Its design allows
a spectrum of projects to be funded in a season.  Superstars, Mega, mini, micro,
crowd pleasers, and even the fringe.

Clearly, all this _may not_ happen without running TRF for a few years. But it is
certainly within the realm of possibility, and certainly not just a dream.  But
best of all, all it needs is ten thousand people under one umbrella - believing
that their 15 rupees can make a change. If that's you, reading this, then please
help us get to just more per season - share our campaign in your network, in your
own words!

Let's go! :rocket: :rocket: :rocket:
